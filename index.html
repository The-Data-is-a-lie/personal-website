Pathfinder Character Sheet

Why

Playing Pathfinder shouldn't cost you anything before you roll a die. I wanted a sheet that anyone could open in a browser and send to a friend, because every other option I found wanted an account first.

How

It is a character sheet built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Nothing gets installed and nothing gets sent to a server. It simply runs inside your browser and saves to your own machine, which is why it is the one project here you can open and click around in right now.

01Why it exists

There was no real Pathfinder 1e character generator. A few existed, but they only rolled some numbers for you and then handed the actual character building back, which is the part that takes all the time. Rolling one by hand took an entire evening, and I wanted it done in seconds.

02Making every character legal, and worth playing

Randomness is the easy part. The hard part is making sure every character it can possibly produce follows every rule, every single time, and that the result is someone you would actually want to play rather than a pile of numbers that happens to be legal.

Pathfinder 1e is one of the most complicated tabletop systems ever published, mostly because almost every choice depends on another choice. One feat requires a different feat first, your class limits which options exist at all, and your ability scores decide what you qualify for. Picking at random produces an illegal character almost every time.

Being legal is not enough on its own. A character can satisfy every requirement in the book and still be miserable to play, like a melee fighter with no Strength. It has to come out coherent.

03Then: two more rule systems, and our own

Handling base Pathfinder 1e was only the first half. We do not play stock. Every character at our table also uses two entire third-party rulebooks, Path of War and Spheres of Power/Might, and both of them replace big pieces of the core game rather than simply adding to it. We have our own house rules on top of that, built up over years of play.

Supporting those two was more work than supporting the base game had been. Neither was written with the other in mind, so the generator constantly has to decide which book wins, and it has to hold two completely different ways of spending a character's resources on one sheet.

Each book also brought its own pile of data. The pools the generator picks from now run to more than 8,800 feats, 6,000 items and 750 classes and archetypes. All of it had to be readable in the same format first.

04The front end I hadn't planned for

Finishing the program did not finish the project. I could produce a correct character and had nowhere to put it, and handing somebody a file full of raw data is not a tool.

So the front end became FoundryVTT, the virtual tabletop my friends and I already played in. That meant learning JavaScript from scratch, as well as learning my way around a very large application somebody else had written. It asks you a few questions and writes the finished character straight into the game.

It is published on the official FoundryVTT package registry, and players have been installing it. Somewhere out there, people are spending their evening on the fun parts of Pathfinder instead of the boring ones. That brings me immense joy.

05What I'd do differently

At the start I did not know what I was doing. I began by copying somebody else's project and changing it piece by piece, and every basic change took far longer than it should have, because I was working inside a structure I did not understand. Eventually I got fed up and rebuilt the whole thing from scratch. That was the right call, although it came much later than it should have.

So the main thing I would change is planning. I would map the whole project out first, including what it needed to do and what data it needed. Almost all of the rework traced back to not having that picture.

The smaller ones are still true. I would keep the rules about what requires what in one place from day one, rather than letting that logic spread into every file. I would also treat the data as the foundation it became. And I would write a test that generates thousands of characters and checks that none of them break a rule.
